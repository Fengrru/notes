Parallel lines never meet. What a pity.

But intersecting lines don't seem much better.

A moment of meeting, then a lifetime drifting apart.

But this metaphor misses one thing.

After two straight lines intersect,

they still extend in their original directions.

That intersection didn't change them.

But people do.

You may not have contacted someone for a long time,

and still love the music they recommended.

When you encounter something,

you still remember how they once put it.

You know this relationship has ended.

So why haven't you returned to who you were before you met?

Return to before you met?

In machine learning, there's a similar hard problem.

We train an AI on data.

Later, if one piece of that data needs to be deleted,

is deleting the original file enough?

It isn't.

Because learning isn't just storing information.

Training changes the parameters inside the model.

The data can be deleted.

But the model, having learned it,

has already become different.

And later learning

continues from this changed place.

> Deleting the data does not undo the learning.

Deleting data is not the same as undoing learning.

So researchers started trying

to make models erase the influence learned from certain data.

Erase that influence?

It's called Machine Unlearning.

A direct but expensive approach

is to remove that data

and retrain on the rest.

What you're trying to recover isn't even the way it used to be,

but the state of never having learned it

while having learned everything else.

Thinking of this,

“If only I had never met you,”

suddenly becomes a very hard question to answer.

Never met you?

If we really had never met,

the songs you'd later hear, the places you'd go,

even the way you understand another person

might all be different.

That self who never met the other person

isn't just missing one memory.

It might be another life.

But that life — we never lived it.

We can remember who we were before we met.

But we don't know what the self who never met

would have become by today.

Maybe one day

that song starts playing in your headphones again.

It's still beautiful.

You finish it, and think it's still beautiful.

And a while later you remember

that it was that person who first played it for you.

You no longer use this song to miss anyone.

But you still love it.

At first, it was something they brought into your life.

Later, it simply became you.

The fact that a person once took part in how you became yourself

doesn't mean he has to keep taking part in your life.

So when I read that line again,

“a moment of meeting, then a lifetime drifting apart,”

I still feel regret.

It's just that people aren't two unchanged straight lines.

You didn't keep walking with me afterward.

You didn't return to the original road.

But I, too, didn't return

to the road from before you came.
